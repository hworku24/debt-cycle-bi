# Debt Cycle Brief - 2026-07

## What Changed This Month

The July data shows a softening economic picture with notable divergences across indicators. The S&P 500 rose to 7,491, marking a 19% year-over-year gain, though momentum has slowed from April's 29.6% peak. The 10-year Treasury yield climbed to 4.58%, up from 4.48% in June, while the yield curve spread (10y-2y) held steady at 0.36%, indicating the inversion has fully reversed.

Inflation continues to moderate with CPI year-over-year at 3.23%, down from June's 3.46%. Money supply (M2) growth remains robust at 5.13% year-over-year, the strongest reading in the trailing period. Consumer credit growth slowed to 1.94% year-over-year from 2.19% in June, suggesting households are pulling back on borrowing. The 30-year mortgage rate edged up to 6.51%, a new high for this tracking period.

Unemployment data for July is not yet available nationally, though June showed 4.2%, the lowest reading since January 2026.

## Cycle Position

The cycle pressure score is unavailable for July due to missing Fed Funds rate data, but June's reading of 0.67 marked the lowest level in over a year, down from 0.86 in July 2025. This decline reflects reduced systemic stress as the Fed has eased rates and debt service burdens have stabilized.

The most recent debt metrics (from January 2026) show the debt service ratio at 11.16% with a z-score of 0.14, indicating near-average historical levels. Federal debt to GDP stands at 122.59% as of January, elevated but stable. Credit card delinquency in January was 2.92% (z-score 0.95), and mortgage delinquency was 1.89%, both manageable and showing slight improvement from prior quarters.

Overall, the cycle appears mid-to-late stage with moderating pressure, not yet signaling imminent distress.

## Regional Notes

State-level unemployment disparities remain significant. The District of Columbia leads at 6.0%, followed by a cluster of West Coast and Northeast states at 5.2% (Oregon, Connecticut, California, Washington). Meanwhile, South Dakota (2.0%), North Dakota (2.3%), and Vermont (2.6%) show very tight labor markets. This 4-percentage-point spread suggests uneven regional economic performance, with coastal and urban areas experiencing softer conditions.

## What To Watch Next Month

Monitor whether consumer sentiment, which has trended down to unmeasured levels in recent months after reading 44.8 in May, shows stabilization or further deterioration. The widening gap between mortgage rates (6.51%) and the 10-year Treasury (4.58%) bears watching as it may constrain housing activity despite home price index growth holding at 1.26% year-over-year.

Updated national unemployment data will be critical to assess whether June's 4.2% represented a turning point. Finally, watch for Q2 2026 debt service and delinquency data (expected in coming months) to confirm whether credit quality continues its gradual improvement.
